Recording that's ready the moment you sign in, saved videos every app accepts, and a quicker sidebar.

### Making a video from your photos

- **Create opens straight away.** Clicking **Create video** (or **Start blank**) now opens a "Making your video" screen at once, instead of leaving you with nothing on screen while it worked. It shows each step, your first photo behind the progress, and your photos along a timeline that light up one by one as each is ready to play. The editor appears when they all are, so every zoom, pan and transition plays smoothly the very first time. In a hurry? **Start editing now** goes straight in.
- **Much faster with phone photos.** Getting a photo ready to play took 20 to 45 seconds for a large phone photo, one photo at a time, which is why effects could be missing the first time a new video played. It now takes about a second each, two at a time: eight photos are ready in about three seconds instead of a minute and a half.
- **Start blank no longer waits.** A new video's empty canvas is made while you're still choosing photos, not after you click.

### Fixes

- **A video kept playing after Briefcast was closed to the tray.** Closing the window now stops whatever was playing: the video, the timeline, the mixer and any preview. It stays paused when you open Briefcast again.

### Recording from the tray

- **Start Briefcast when you sign in.** A new setting (Settings › Appearance › Closing the window) starts Briefcast in the tray without opening its window, so **Ctrl+Shift+R**, the tray menu and the phone Remote work from the moment you sign in. The window opens when you click the tray icon.
- **Free memory while in the tray.** Turn this on and, after a minute in the tray, Briefcast closes its window to give back memory, then opens it again in a second or two when you click the tray icon. Recording, the shortcuts and the phone Remote keep working while it's closed. It waits while an export, the live display, a voice-over take or a phone-camera recording is running. Off unless you turn it on.
- **Your recording setup is remembered.** The camera bubble's shape, position and size stay as you set them between launches, and the shortcut and the tray record with the screen, camera and microphone you last picked. If your usual microphone or camera is unplugged, Briefcast uses another for now and goes back to yours when it's plugged in again.
- **The 3, 2, 1 countdown** now also runs before recordings started from the shortcut or the tray.
- **Shortcuts belong to Briefcast, not its window,** so they keep working while the window is closed or reloading, and a shortcut another app already uses is reported as soon as Briefcast starts.

### Saving videos

- **YouTube and WhatsApp / Instagram choices** in the Save button's quality picker. YouTube keeps the full size and frame rate at high quality, since YouTube compresses every upload again. WhatsApp / Instagram makes a 1080p, 30 fps file that WhatsApp Status and Reels accept: a 4K, 60 fps export used to be refused there as "format not supported".
- **No more washed-out saved videos.** Screen recordings can carry a colour range some players, TVs and upload sites show washed out, with crushed blacks, or refuse. Every saved video now uses the standard range.

### Library

- **Sort a folder by name, newest first or oldest first** with the sort button on the folder's row in the sidebar. Each folder keeps its own order, so your recordings can show the latest take on top while other folders stay A to Z.
- **Double-click Home** to go straight to the main home screen from anywhere.

### Faster

- **Large folders open at once.** The sidebar shows the first items straight away and fills in the rest right after, and thumbnails you've already seen come back instantly when you reopen it.
- **The effects and transitions galleries open and scroll instantly.** Every preview is drawn once, and only the one under your pointer plays.

### Your settings

Settings now live in a file of their own rather than in the window's storage, which is what lets the shortcuts and the tray work with no window open. Yours are carried over automatically the first time you open this version.

### macOS and Linux

Briefcast now builds and runs from source on macOS and Linux. There are no installers for them yet. On those systems it uses the ffmpeg already installed and tells you at startup if that version is too old. The project's README lists what each system needs.
